# Source Library — "How to Build a Real-Time Internet Lead Team"

**Compiled:** 2026-06-15 · **Audience:** B2C high-consideration verticals (mortgage, insurance, solar, education)
**Purpose:** The verified evidence base for the flagship report. Every claim that ships in the report traces to a row here. This doubles as the report's public **Methodology & Sources** section.

## How to read this file
- **Tier** = how much weight a figure can carry:
  - `ANCHOR` — independent or large-N, recent (2024–2026), safe to headline.
  - `STRONG/VENDOR` — large-sample and current but the publisher sells a related product; cite *with* the vendor named.
  - `DIRECTIONAL` — single point, small sample, or thin sourcing; use as color, never as the backbone.
  - `HISTORICAL` — the foundational 2007–2015 canon; cite as the *origin of the idea*, always date-stamped, never as current proof.
- **Domain** = the field the data is actually from (sales / CX / telecom / marketing / consumer / legal). High-consideration B2C extrapolations from B2B are labeled.

## 1. The methodology problem (state this openly in the report)

There is **no rigorous, recent, independent, vertical-specific speed-to-lead conversion study** in mortgage, insurance, solar, or education. The decay numbers everyone quotes ("respond in 5 minutes → 21× / 100× / 391%") are **2007–2015, cross-industry, and mostly vendor-published.** Sales-specific research is thin; consumer research is richer. So this report:
1. Demotes the old canon to "origin of the idea," date-stamped.
2. Anchors on current (2024–2026) consumer + telecom + CX data and on independent vertical sources (J.D. Power, LendingTree, NerdWallet/Harris, Gallup, Pew).
3. Borrows B2B sales-ops rigor explicitly labeled as "learn-from," because high-consideration consumer purchases (financed, comparison-shopped, trust-driven) behave more like B2B than like impulse retail.
4. Builds **trend lines** (a slope across years) rather than single point-estimates wherever possible — a rising slope is the emerging signal even when the definitive study doesn't exist.

## 9. DO NOT CITE — folklore & misattributions (kill on sight)

- **"MBA: mortgage lead conversion is 3–5%"** — no primary Mortgage Bankers Association source exists.
- **"78% of buyers go with the first responder"** — no traceable primary; InsideSales-lineage marketing stat.
- **"Texting lifts contact rate ~300% / 3×"** (attributed to EverQuote) — unverifiable.
- **"80% of leads that don't convert immediately eventually buy"** — folklore; use Gleanster "50% qualified but not ready" + Forrester nurture lift instead.
- **"79% never convert *because of* no nurturing"** — the causal tail is invented; the 79% (MarketingSherpa) is real, the "because" is not.
- **"21× / 100× / 5-minute rule" attributed to Harvard Business Review** — misattribution. Those are the MIT/InsideSales numbers; HBR's are 7× and 60×.
- **"~55% prefer phone for mortgages / ~64% healthcare"** — unverified; use Hiya 42% healthcare (2025) / 37% banking (2023).
- **AgentZap solar "loses 80% value in 5 min / 47-hr avg / phone 3×"** — attributed only to unnamed "industry studies."
